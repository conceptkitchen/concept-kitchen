## The Truth No One Tells You

There's a skill that makes AI (and people) actually useful. Nobody teaches it. But once you see it, you can't unsee it.

It's this: how to ask for what you want. Clearly. Specifically. In a way that actually gets results.

Most of us were never taught this. We say what we *mean*, but they hear something else. We get frustrated. They get confused. The result is garbage.

Sound familiar?

Here's the thing: AI just made this gap impossible to ignore.

When you ask a person something vague, they might figure it out. They'll fill in the gaps. They'll ask follow-up questions. They'll use context clues.

AI doesn't do that. AI takes your vague request and gives you exactly what you asked for — which is usually not what you wanted.

**Vague in. Garbage out.**

But here's the flip side: when you learn to ask clearly, AI becomes the most useful tool you've ever touched. And that same skill? It makes every other conversation in your life better too.

This isn't a prompt engineering guide. This is a communication guide.

AI is just where we're going to practice.

---

## Why Your Requests Fail

Most people's requests fail for three reasons:

### 1. You assume shared context

You know what you mean. You've been thinking about this for hours. Days. Weeks.

The person (or AI) you're talking to? They just walked in. They don't have your context. They don't know your preferences. They don't know what you tried before.

**You're starting at chapter 7. They're starting at page 1.**

When you skip the context, they fill in the blanks with their own assumptions. And their assumptions aren't yours.

### 2. You ask for too much at once

"Can you help me with my presentation? I need it to be engaging but professional, about 10 slides, covering our Q3 results and next quarter's strategy, and make sure to include the Johnson account story but don't make it too long, and I need it by tomorrow."

That's not a request. That's a brain dump.

Good requests are focused. One clear thing at a time. If you need multiple things, break them up.

### 3. You're vague about what "good" looks like

"Make it better."
"I want it to pop."
"Just clean it up."

What does "better" mean? Better how? More concise? More detailed? More formal? More casual?

If you can't describe what success looks like, you can't expect anyone to deliver it.

## The Anatomy of a Clear Request

Every clear request has four parts:

### 1. CONTEXT — Who you are and what's happening

Before you ask for anything, set the scene.

- What's the situation?
- What's your role?
- What have you already tried?
- What constraints exist?

**Bad:** "Write me an email."

**Good:** "I'm a project manager. I need to tell my team that the deadline moved up by two weeks. They're already stressed. I want to deliver the news firmly but without causing panic."

The context changes everything.

### 2. SPECIFICS — What exactly you want

Be precise. Painfully precise.

- What format?
- What length?
- What's included?
- What's the goal?

**Bad:** "Make it short."

**Good:** "Keep it under 150 words. Three paragraphs max."

**Bad:** "Give me some ideas."

**Good:** "Give me 5 specific ideas for [X]. For each one, include a one-sentence description and why it might work."

### 3. CONSTRAINTS — What to avoid or stay within

Tell them where the guardrails are.

- What tone to avoid?
- What not to include?
- What style to match?
- What's off-limits?

**Example:** "Don't use corporate jargon. No bullet points. Keep the tone conversational like I'm talking to a colleague, not presenting to executives."

Constraints prevent the most common screwups.

### 4. EXAMPLE — Show, don't just tell (when possible)

If you can, show what good looks like.

- "Here's an email I wrote before that I liked. Match this tone."
- "Here's the format I want. Follow this structure."
- "This is what I DON'T want. Avoid this approach."

Examples eliminate ambiguity faster than explanations.

## The Before & After

Let's see this in action.

### Example 1: The Email

**Before (vague):**
> "Write an email to my team about the project delay."

**After (clear):**
> "I'm a project manager at a marketing agency. Our website redesign project is delayed by 3 weeks because the client keeps changing requirements.
>
> Write an email to my team (5 people) delivering this news. Keep it under 200 words. Tone should be direct but not blaming — I don't want them to feel like they failed. End with a clear next step.
>
> Avoid corporate buzzwords. Sound like a real person."

**Why it works:** Context (role, situation, cause). Specifics (length, audience, goal). Constraints (tone, what to avoid). Clear outcome.

---

### Example 2: The Brainstorm

**Before (vague):**
> "Give me ideas for my business."

**After (clear):**
> "I run a small landscaping company in Phoenix. Most of our clients are residential homeowners, 40-65 years old. We do well in spring but slow down in summer when it's too hot for yard work.
>
> Give me 5 ideas for services or offers we could promote during the slow summer months. For each idea, give me one sentence explaining it and one sentence on why Phoenix homeowners specifically might want it."

**Why it works:** Specific business, specific audience, specific problem, specific format for the answer.

---

### Example 3: The Life Request (Non-AI)

This works on people too.

**Before (vague):**
> "Can you help me with dinner?"

**After (clear):**
> "I'm making pasta tonight. Can you chop the onions and garlic while I boil the water? The cutting board is out. Should take about 5 minutes."

**Why it works:** Specific task, specific location, time expectation. No ambiguity.

Same skill. Different context.

## The 5-Point Check (Before You Ask Anything)

Use this before every request — AI or human.

### ✓ 1. Did I set the context?
What's the situation? What do they need to know to help me?

### ✓ 2. Did I say exactly what I want?
Not vaguely. Specifically. What format? What length? What's the goal?

### ✓ 3. Did I tell them what to avoid?
What tone, style, or content would make this wrong?

### ✓ 4. Did I give an example (if helpful)?
Can I show them what good looks like?

### ✓ 5. Is this one clear request — or am I asking for 5 things at once?
If it's more than one thing, break it up.

## Put It to Work: Try This Now

Here's your practice. Open ChatGPT (or any AI). Try this prompt:

---

**The Prompt:**

> I'm a [YOUR ROLE] who needs to [YOUR TASK]. 
>
> Context: [2-3 sentences about your situation]
>
> What I need: [Specific deliverable — format, length, goal]
>
> Constraints: [What to avoid or stay within]
>
> Here's an example of what I'm looking for: [Optional — include if you have one]

---

Fill in your own details. See what you get.

Then try asking the same thing without the structure. Compare the results.

The difference is the skill.

## Pro Tips: For When You Want to Be Smartly Lazy

You've learned the framework. Now here's how to make AI do the heavy lifting for you.

---

### Pro Tip #1: Make AI Ask YOU the Questions

Don't want to remember the whole framework every time? Make AI pull it out of you.

**Copy this instruction into ChatGPT Custom Instructions or paste it at the start of any chat:**

```
Before responding to any request I give you:

1. Ask me clarifying questions to understand the full context
2. Keep asking until you have:
   - My role / who I am
   - The situation / background
   - The goal / what I'm trying to achieve
   - The audience / who this is for
   - Any constraints / what to avoid
3. Summarize what you understood and ask me to confirm
4. Only after I confirm, give me your response

If I'm being vague, push back and ask for specifics. Don't guess.
```

**What happens:**
- You say "I need help with an email"
- AI asks: "Who's it to? What's the context? What tone?"
- You answer naturally
- AI summarizes: "So you want X for Y, avoiding Z. Correct?"
- You confirm
- AI delivers something actually useful

No more remembering. No more garbage outputs.

---

### Pro Tip #2: Attach This File to Your Conversation

Even lazier? Just upload this PDF (or save it as a .txt file) to your AI conversation.

Tell AI: "Use this as your guide for how to handle my requests."

The AI will reference the framework throughout the whole conversation. You don't have to memorize anything or paste instructions. Just attach and go.

**Works with:**
- ChatGPT (file upload)
- Claude (file upload)
- Any AI that accepts attachments

This guide isn't just something to read — it's a tool you can use.
