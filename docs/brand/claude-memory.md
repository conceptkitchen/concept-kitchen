# Claude's Memory of The Concept Kitchen Project

*Extracted from Claude export — January 2026*

## Purpose & Context

RJ is building The Concept Kitchen, an AI education brand that teaches non-technical professionals to become AI builders using cooking metaphors. The core philosophy is "anybody can cook" - meaning people don't need formal credentials, CS degrees, or chef-level expertise to build with AI tools. RJ's authentic background as both a literal cook (ran a restaurant pop-up for 8-9 months) and self-taught AI builder (scaled from employee #30 to Director at YC startup Magic, won multiple hackathons, built healthcare apps) serves as the brand's proof point against tech gatekeeping culture.

The target audience includes construction professionals, business owners, realtors, and other non-technical workers experiencing AI impostor syndrome, credential anxiety, and fear of being left behind. Success means transforming these professionals from intimidated observers into confident AI builders who see themselves as capable of creating solutions. The brand sells identity transformation and permission rather than just information, positioning against boring corporate AI training and dismissive tech culture.

Key constraints include maintaining the "cook not chef" positioning (never claiming formal expertise), staying accessible to time-constrained professionals (most have only 24 minutes per week for learning), and building systematic marketing infrastructure using the Northbound Labs framework. The approach emphasizes emotional safety, practical recipes over theory, and role-specific applications that resonate with each persona's daily challenges.

## Key Learnings & Principles

The cooking metaphor positioning is completely unoccupied in the AI education market, creating a significant first-mover advantage. Research confirms that role-specific content achieves 40-60% higher engagement than generic AI training, and interactive content converts 70% higher than static formats.

Key audience insights include that 56% of non-technical workers experience AI impostor syndrome, 77.5% feel overwhelmed by too many tools, and 52% fear being left behind. The brand must sell identity transformation and belonging rather than just information, addressing emotional barriers before technical ones.

Sales psychology principles reveal the importance of positioning against gatekeeping culture rather than other AI educators, similar to how Liquid Death succeeded by selling rebellion rather than hydration. The Fogg Behavior Model (Behavior = Motivation + Ability + Prompt) guides content strategy by increasing motivation through identity transformation while decreasing friction through familiar recipe formats.

---

## Approach & Patterns

RJ follows the systematic Northbound Labs marketing framework, implementing a structured approach to brand building, audience research, content strategy, and funnel development. The methodology emphasizes completing foundational research before content creation, ensuring all messaging aligns with documented brand guidelines and persona insights.

Content strategy uses the 40/40/20 ratio across TOFU/MOFU/BOFU stages, with growth content featuring viral hooks and emotional connection, nurture content providing practical tips and behind-the-scenes access, and sales content using carousels and clear CTAs. All content maintains the "cook not chef" positioning, never claiming formal expertise while demonstrating practical building capabilities.

The brand consistently applies sales psychology principles of emotional connection, friction reduction, and motivation increase. Communication style balances warm accessibility with energetic expertise, avoiding both intimidating guru positioning and overly casual approaches that might undermine credibility.
